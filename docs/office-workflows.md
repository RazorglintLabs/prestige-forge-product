# Prestige Forge for professional review work

**Proposed buyer promise: know what supports the words you hand over.**

Prestige Forge's Office Review Desk brings statements, exact source references, limitations, owners and reviewer notes into a local matter record. Its practical job is to reduce reconstruction before a client update, internal review or handoff. This is an evaluation workflow, not a claim of validated market demand.

| Team | Start with one real task | Useful output | Professional judgment stays with |
|---|---|---|---|
| Lawyers and paralegals | A client update or chronology with disputed/missing support | Statement-to-clause/page register, limitations and unresolved questions | The responsible legal professional |
| Accountants and bookkeepers | A period-end explanation or review workpaper | Figure/statement-to-cell references, period scope and reviewer notes | The responsible accountant or reviewer |
| Operations and office teams | A supplier or project handoff | Recorded status, acceptance references, open owners and due dates | The responsible operator |

## What the evaluation build does

- Separate matters with client/engagement references and explicit scope.
- Legal, accounting and office prompts; exact statements entered by the user.
- Local source copies, SHA-256 fingerprints, and precise page/clause/cell references.
- A required explanation of why each source supports each statement.
- Human review with a name, rationale and expiry date. Changes and expired evidence withdraw readiness.
- A handoff ZIP with printable HTML, spreadsheet CSV, JSON and checksums. Raw source documents are excluded by default.
- Open questions and limitations remain visible. A missing source cannot be skipped by clicking review.

Readiness means a matching human review is recorded. It is not an automated finding of legal safety, financial accuracy or factual truth. The existing developer proof engine and the office human-review workflow have distinct meanings.

## A five-minute buyer demonstration

Use fictional documents first. Create a supplier-agreement matter, enter a notice-period statement and a broad unsupported completion statement. Attach a short fictional agreement. Link the notice statement to the exact clause and preserve its exception. Record a review. Show one ready statement and one unresolved statement. Change “30 days” to “60 days”: readiness disappears. Export the register with the unresolved item still visible.

The buyer should understand the value without knowing Git, JSON schemas or proof-engine module names. Ask whether this makes their existing review task easier. Do not promise fewer errors or time savings before measuring them.

## What should be sold first

Offer a **bounded assisted evaluation of one recurring review workflow** to a small legal, accounting or professional-services team. Define the input documents, number of statements, responsible reviewer, intended recipient, output formats and review cycle in advance. Keep documents on the client's approved device where possible. Agree confidentiality and handling terms before accessing client records.

Price discovery is still needed. Do not present an invented subscription price or guaranteed ROI as market evidence. Record their current preparation/review time, time with the desk, missing references found, reviewer usability, willingness to repeat the workflow and willingness to pay. An evaluation is successful when the buyer voluntarily wants to use it on the next comparable matter and accepts a concrete paid offer.

Suggested packaging to validate: a local individual desk; an assisted firm evaluation with workflow setup; later a firm licence only once authenticated review, deployment, updates and support are ready. Existing developer tiers remain available separately; the office build does not enforce paid licences.

## What must be finished before a broad paid release

1. Test the complete workflow with target professionals and establish willingness to pay.
2. Validate on real Windows machines; supply a signed installer, update path and recovery instructions.
3. Prove backup/restore, retention and the chosen operating model with realistic confidential records under approved handling.
4. Complete focused security and accessibility review; settle commercial, support and licence terms.
5. Add collaboration, document-system integration or extraction only when repeated buyer evidence justifies it.
